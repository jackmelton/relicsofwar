Research Standards

Last reviewed 2026-08-15

Identification and historical pages on Relics of War aim to be the kind of reference a serious collector would keep on the shelf. That means:

Correct nomenclature

We use the terminology of the period and of the standard references, and we use it consistently: the same name for the same pattern, maker, unit, battle, or artifact type across every page. Names are tied to ArtifactSearch's controlled vocabulary so that what you read here matches what you search for there.

What a good guide covers

Where the material supports it, a guide addresses: the historical period and military use; the maker and manufacturing details; known variations, markings, and inspection marks; dimensions, caliber, and materials; provenance considerations; authentication points; known reproductions and how to recognize them; collector terminology; comparable examples; and the references consulted.

What we will not do

  • Present an attribution, provenance, or rarity claim we cannot support.
  • Estimate a value for a specific piece from a photograph.
  • Declare an item authentic or fake. Our guides teach what to look for; the judgment on a particular object belongs to a qualified examiner with the object in hand.

Reproductions

Many of the most collected artifacts are also the most reproduced. Guides on this site call out known reproductions and cast copies specifically, because the single most valuable thing an identification guide can do is stop a bad purchase.

Sources we rely on

Official Records · ordnance and quartermaster manuals · period catalogs · manufacturer records · museum collections · auction archives · the published reference literature. Where a guide draws on a specific work, it says so.
