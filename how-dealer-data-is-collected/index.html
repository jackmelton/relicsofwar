How Dealer Data Is Collected

Last reviewed 2026-08-15

Who appears

The listings behind Relics of War come from the dealers and auction houses that participate in ArtifactSearch.com. Every source is a United States business, reviewed before it is activated, and a source that is found to sell reproductions as originals is not added — or is removed. Sources are identified on every listing card and on their own profile pages on ArtifactSearch.

How listings are gathered

ArtifactSearch reads each participating source's public website or catalog on a schedule (nightly for most), records each listing's title, description, price, photographs, and category, and links back to the original page. Sources may also supply listings directly. Nothing is edited into a listing that the seller did not publish; category and era assignments are added so listings can be organized and searched.

Sensitive material

Some historical material is handled with additional care. Items in restricted or under-review sensitive categories are not syndicated to Relics of War at all, even where ArtifactSearch shows them behind its own gating.

Refresh cycle

Relics of War rebuilds every night from a read-only copy of ArtifactSearch's public catalog. New listings appear the next morning; sold or withdrawn listings disappear the same way. Sold results feed the price guide.

What is excluded

  • Sources that are paused, retired, or hidden on ArtifactSearch.
  • Listings that are sold, withdrawn, or awaiting review.
  • Modern (post-1975) military surplus, reproductions and reenactor supplies, metal detectors, and books, which have their own sections on ArtifactSearch and are not part of Relics of War's scope.
